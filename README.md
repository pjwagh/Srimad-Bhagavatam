
## Why Devotees Rise Early For Krishna | Sri Amitasana Dasa | SB 3.22.33

 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZtAd4CxRT7Y

## Central theme

Human life is a valuable opportunity for spiritual progress. We should use it to regulate material desires through **dharma**, strengthen our consciousness through **sādhana**, and participate sincerely in the mission of the Lord and the spiritual master.

> **“Choose your battles wisely. Time is precious.”**

The lecture reminds us that time, energy, and attention are limited. We should not allow Māyā to pull us into unnecessary arguments, distractions, conflicts, and sense gratification.

---

## 1. Human life is meant for progress

The opening story describes a person whose ear was swollen after he mistakenly picked up a hot iron when the telephone rang. Later, when another telephone rang, he repeated the same mistake with his other ear.

The lesson is that human beings should learn from their mistakes. Repeating the same mistake means that we are not making progress.

Human birth is not meant merely for eating, sleeping, working, and pursuing temporary pleasures. It is meant for gradual spiritual advancement.

### Main point

- Learn from past mistakes.
- Improve our habits and consciousness.
- Use human intelligence for spiritual progress.

---

## 2. Why does the material world exist?

According to the lecture, the living entities have come to the material world because of material desires and envy of the Lord. However, the Lord is merciful and gives living entities an opportunity to exercise their free will.

The material world is therefore not without purpose. It provides a place where living entities can try to fulfil their desires, but they are encouraged to do so according to **dharmic principles**.

Krishna respects free will. The living entity may pursue material goals, but the proper way to do so is by following dharma.

### Important understanding

The Lord does not force us. He gives us freedom, guidance, and repeated opportunities to return to spiritual life.

---

## 3. Happiness is not the same as comfort

Everyone wants to be happy. People search for happiness through nightlife, travel, adventure, food, entertainment, technology, and material facilities.

However, comfort and happiness are not directly correlated. A person may have wealth, luxury, and every material facility yet still feel empty or disturbed.

The lecture compares modern life with earlier times, when people often enjoyed refined forms of music, such as classical music and traditional instruments like the **sitar** and **shehnai**. Material advancement does not automatically produce inner happiness.

### Main point

External facilities can make life comfortable, but lasting happiness comes from a peaceful and spiritually connected consciousness.

---

## 4. Dharma gives direction to material life

There is no prohibition against family life, wealth, or material enjoyment. The important question is how these things are pursued.

Even a person living with a wife and children can live a progressive life if the household is conducted according to religious principles.

Without dharma, human life becomes similar to animal life. The Sanskrit principle quoted in the lecture is:

> **Dharma-hīnaḥ paśubhiḥ samānaḥ**  
> A person without dharma is like an animal.

The traditional progression is:

1. **Dharma** — living according to spiritual and moral principles
2. **Artha** — obtaining resources responsibly
3. **Kāma** — fulfilling legitimate desires in a regulated manner
4. **Mokṣa** — progressing toward liberation

Dharma is the foundation. If dharma is removed, the purpose of human life is lost.

### Practical understanding

- Dharma helps us use resources properly.
- Artha provides necessary facilities.
- Regulated kāma prevents uncontrolled sense gratification.
- The ultimate goal is mokṣa and spiritual realization.

---

## 5. The example of Svāyambhuva Manu

The lecture refers to Emperor **Svāyambhuva Manu** as an example of someone who lived a responsible household life while remaining connected to the Lord.

His life demonstrates that spiritual life is not limited to renouncing family responsibilities. A household can become Krishna conscious when its daily activities are centred around hearing, chanting, worship, and service.

In the example discussed, musicians would sing about the glories of the Lord in the morning, and the emperor and his family would hear about the Lord’s pastimes. The day began in a spiritually uplifting atmosphere.

### Lesson for householders

Family life should not be separated from spiritual life. The home should become a place where family members:

- Hear about Krishna
- Chant together
- Read Bhagavad-gītā or Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam
- Take prasādam
- Support one another in devotional service

---

## 6. The importance of morning sādhana

The early morning is a particularly valuable time for spiritual practice. The recommended routine includes:

- Rising early
- Taking a bath and getting ready
- Attending **Mangalārati**
- Chanting the holy name
- Reading or hearing spiritual literature
- Offering prayers
- Performing service

The lecture acknowledges that devotees may face health problems, insomnia, lack of sleep, or difficult circumstances. These realities should be approached with understanding and practical judgment. Nevertheless, whenever possible, we should protect the morning and make the best use of it.

### Why morning practice matters

The world is full of distractions, passion, ignorance, advertisements, entertainment, and opportunities for sense gratification. Morning sādhana gives us spiritual strength before entering these influences.

If we begin the day with Krishna consciousness, we are better prepared to deal with stress, temptations, negative thoughts, and unnecessary discussions or conflicts.

Morning sādhana is like charging a battery. The spiritual strength gained in the morning helps us throughout the day.

---

## 7. The spiritual atmosphere of the temple

The temple is not necessarily silent in the ordinary sense. There may be kīrtana, classes, conversations, worship, and other activities. Still, people often experience peace when they sit in a temple.

The reason given is the spiritual atmosphere created by:

- Chanting the holy name
- Kīrtana and saṅkīrtana
- Deity worship
- Ārati
- Classes and discussions about Krishna
- Association with devotees

The temple hall becomes spiritually charged through regular devotional activities. When many devotees chant, worship, and hear together, the atmosphere benefits everyone who enters.

We should make use of this atmosphere rather than remaining distracted. Chanting with devotees, especially during the early morning and Brahma-muhūrta, can be especially powerful.

---

## 8. “Choose your battles wisely”

Māyā often engages us in unnecessary battles. Someone says something, we feel the need to justify ourselves, and a small matter becomes a long argument.

The mind then becomes absorbed in proving that we are right, resolving conflicts that did not need to exist, and repeatedly analysing someone else’s words.

We have limited time and limited energy. We cannot fight every battle or respond to every comment.

> **Choose your battles wisely. Time is precious—do not waste it on things that do not deserve your energy.**

### Questions to ask before reacting

1. Is this issue genuinely important?
2. Will responding improve the situation?
3. Is my response based on service and truth, or on ego?
4. Will this discussion help my spiritual life?
5. Can I forgive, let go, and move forward?

---

## 9. Keep life simple

Many complications in life are created by the mind. We may say that life is complex, but often the complexity comes from unnecessary desires, reactions, expectations, and conflicts.

The lecture gives a simple formula:

> **Chant Hare Krishna and be happy.**

Simple spiritual habits include:

- Chanting the Hare Krishna mahā-mantra
- Taking prasādam
- Hearing Bhagavad-gītā
- Hearing Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam
- Reading Śrīla Prabhupāda’s books
- Associating with devotees
- Performing sincere service

Simplicity does not mean irresponsibility. It means keeping the real purpose of life clear and avoiding unnecessary mental burdens.

---

## 10. The “sandwich” program: begin and end the day properly

The way we end the previous day influences how we begin the next day. Similarly, our state of consciousness before sleeping affects our thoughts, sleep, and morning routine.

The lecture recommends using the final part of the day for kīrtana, prayer, reading Bhagavad-gītā or Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, hearing a spiritual class, remembering the Lord, and expressing gratitude.

Ideally, the last fifteen minutes before sleep should be free from disturbing content. Reels, Instagram, YouTube, news, and other stimulating material late at night can disturb the mind and make it difficult to wake up peacefully.

### Suggested evening routine

1. Reduce screen use before bedtime.
2. Spend time with family in kīrtana or spiritual discussion.
3. Read or hear something about Krishna.
4. Offer prayers and express gratitude.
5. Sleep at a suitable time.

The consciousness with which we sleep often influences the consciousness with which we wake up.

---

## 11. We are fortunate to have this opportunity

Many people are so burdened by their circumstances that, by the end of the day, they are completely exhausted. They may not have the opportunity to peacefully hear Bhagavad-gītā, hear Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, chant, and sleep in a spiritual atmosphere.

Those who have such an opportunity should not take it for granted.

We may have the opportunity to wake up early, attend Mangalārati, chant rounds, read spiritual books, hear Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, serve in the temple, and live in the association of devotees.

Missing these opportunities repeatedly and then giving excuses requires honest introspection. We should recognize our good fortune and use it properly.

### Gratitude instead of entitlement

When we forget our good fortune, entitlement and expectation increase:

- “Why did he not respect me?”
- “Why did he speak to me like that?”
- “Why did I not receive what I expected?”

Humility changes the perspective:

> “I am fallen, yet the Lord has given me so many facilities. I should do my part sincerely.”

The Lord remains compassionate despite our mistakes and shortcomings.

---

## 12. The temple and devotional service create lasting benefit

A temple creates opportunities for people to connect with the Lord, hear about Krishna, learn about the holy name, experience a Krishna-conscious atmosphere, and begin or strengthen their spiritual life.

This opportunity is not limited to one person or one generation. The facility created through devotional service may benefit thousands or even millions of people over many years.

Material facilities such as malls, theatres, clubs, and gyms provide temporary benefits. Facilities created for Krishna’s service provide spiritual benefit, and devotional service itself is eternal.

### Do not lose the big picture

Responsibilities such as fundraising, administration, construction, and management may be necessary. However, they are only parts of a much larger mission.

The larger purpose is to help people receive an opportunity to connect with the Lord. We should not become so absorbed in money, anxiety, or daily tasks that we forget this purpose.

---

## 13. Spiritual advancement means participating in the mission

Spiritual advancement does not come only from memorizing verses or collecting information. It comes from understanding the mission of the Lord and the spiritual master and participating in that mission.

Participation may require sacrificing personal comfort, personal preferences, likes, dislikes, time, and convenience.

When we serve sincerely, we receive the mercy of the Lord and the spiritual master.

> **If we are not serving Krishna, we are serving Māyā. There is no third option.**

If we are not glorifying Krishna, our time will usually be absorbed in gross or subtle sense gratification. Therefore, we should consciously choose service.

---

## 14. There is no loss in devotional service

Even if we do not complete our spiritual journey in this life, devotional progress is not wasted. Whatever spiritual benefit has been gained continues, and in the next life one can continue from that point.

Therefore:

- Every sincere effort has value.
- Every person helped by devotional service benefits eternally.
- Every opportunity created for someone to hear or chant has lasting significance.
- We should not become discouraged by imperfect progress.

This opportunity is described as the mercy of Krishna and Śrīla Prabhupāda.

## Practical daily plan

### Morning

- Sleep early enough to rise on time.
- Wake up and prepare without unnecessary delay.
- Attend Mangalārati whenever possible.
- Chant the holy name and complete the required rounds.
- Read or hear Bhagavad-gītā or Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam.
- Offer prayers and perform service.

### During the day

- Remember the real purpose of human life.
- Protect the mind from unnecessary media and distractions.
- Avoid avoidable arguments.
- Choose battles wisely.
- Keep responsibilities connected to service.
- Maintain gratitude instead of entitlement.

### Evening

- Hear or read spiritual literature.
- Spend time with family in kīrtana or prayer.
- Reduce screen use, especially during the final fifteen minutes.
- Remember Krishna before sleeping.
- Prepare to begin the next day with spiritual consciousness.

## Key takeaways

1. Human life is meant for progress, not repeated mistakes.
2. The material world gives us an opportunity to use free will responsibly.
3. Dharma is the foundation for a progressive life.
4. Comfort, wealth, and technology do not guarantee happiness.
5. Family life can be spiritual when it is centred on Krishna.
6. Morning sādhana gives spiritual strength for the entire day.
7. The temple atmosphere is strengthened by chanting, worship, hearing, and devotee association.
8. Do not waste limited time and energy on unnecessary battles.
9. A simple life centred on chanting, hearing, prasādam, and service is spiritually powerful.
10. The way we end the day influences how we begin the next day.
11. We should recognize our good fortune and serve with humility.
12. Devotional service creates eternal benefit and is never wasted.
13. If we are not serving Krishna, we are serving Māyā.

## Memorable conclusion

We have received a rare and valuable opportunity: to wake up, chant, hear about Krishna, associate with devotees, and serve the mission of the Lord. We should not treat this opportunity as ordinary. By using it properly, keeping life simple, choosing our battles wisely, and remaining engaged in devotional service, we can make genuine spiritual progress.

**Hare Krishna.**
