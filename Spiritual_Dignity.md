# Spiritual Dignity — Notes
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HqSlj6jqJ6s
A Devotee Must Preserve His Dignity | Sri Amitasana Dasa | SB 3.22.13

## Central Message

Spiritual dignity means remembering that we are devotees of Krishna and living, speaking, thinking, and acting in a way that reflects that identity.

It is not about personal pride, social status, or what we personally like and dislike. It is about the standard, responsibility, and conduct expected from a devotee who represents Krishna and Srila Prabhupada.

## 1. What Is Dignity?

Everyone has some idea of dignity, but dignity differs from person to person.

For example:

- Some people may be comfortable sleeping on a railway platform when a train is delayed.
- For another person, sleeping on the platform may feel below their dignity.
- Some people may consider travelling by train completely normal.
- A famous person or senior official may expect business-class travel or special treatment.

This shows that dignity is relative. A person behaves according to the standard they identify with.

### Main lesson

A devotee must understand the dignity connected with spiritual life. The question is not only, “What am I comfortable doing?” The question is, “Is this behavior suitable for a devotee?”

## 2. Respectable Marriage and Spiritual Dignity

The lecture begins with the example of Manu and Kardamuni.

The general procedure of Vedic marriage is that a father offers his daughter to a suitable boy. A boy should not go to the girl's father and ask for the hand of his daughter in marriage.

This is presented as a matter of maintaining a respectable position.

### Main lesson

Spiritual dignity means understanding the appropriate standard for our position and behaving accordingly.

## 3. The Spiritual Dignity of a Devotee

A person wearing devotional clothes is immediately noticed by others.

In a train, many people may be wearing ordinary shirts and pants. A devotee with a shaved head, shikha, and devotional dress stands out. People notice the devotee's behavior, speech, conduct, and even the way the devotee eats.

People expect a sadhu or devotee to be:

- Calm
- Gentle
- Soft-spoken
- Self-controlled
- Tolerant
- Free from unnecessary anger
- Clean and truthful

Wearing devotional dress may take only a short time, but maintaining the spiritual dignity connected with that dress is difficult.

### Main lesson

The dress represents a spiritual identity. Therefore, our conduct should be worthy of the identity we are representing.

> “Therefore, it is important for all of us to maintain that spiritual dignity.”

## 4. Code of Conduct and Public Behavior

A devotee's behavior is observed by the public. People form an impression of the guru and the spiritual tradition through the devotee's conduct.

The lecture explains that there is a code of conduct for devotees. It gives the example that devotees should avoid eating in public places, especially when surrounded by people.

The general public expects a spiritual person to behave differently from an ordinary person. At times, devotees may need to manage crowds or situations firmly, and anger may have an appropriate place. However, the general standard remains calmness, self-control, tolerance, and good behavior.

### Main lesson

Before speaking or acting in public, a devotee should remember:

- People are observing the behavior.
- The behavior may influence how they view the guru.
- The behavior may influence how they view Krishna consciousness.
- Personal reactions should not replace spiritual responsibility.

## 5. Cleanliness and Spiritual Dignity

The lecture describes an incident involving Srila Prabhupada in Mayapur.

Many devotees had come for the Gaur Purnima festival. Temporary toilets had been arranged, but they became extremely dirty and clogged. Srila Prabhupada became upset and questioned how the managers could tolerate such conditions.

The lecture then explains that our reaction to an unclean environment reveals something about our consciousness.

If a person is comfortable living in a dirty room, using an unclean bed, or staying in an unclean place without any reaction, there may be a problem with the person's consciousness.

A devotee is expected to be clean and truthful. Living comfortably in a filthy or unclean environment is not consistent with that standard.

### Main lesson

Cleanliness is not merely a matter of personal preference. For a devotee, maintaining cleanliness is a duty connected with spiritual dignity.

> “It is our duty to maintain that standard.”

## 6. Personal Preference Versus Spiritual Duty

A person may say:

- “It is okay with me.”
- “It does not matter to me.”
- “I am comfortable with it.”

But a devotee must look beyond personal comfort.

The important question is not whether something is personally liked or disliked. The important question is whether it is suitable for someone representing Krishna and Srila Prabhupada.

### Main lesson

Spiritual life requires us to replace the attitude of “I care only about my preference” with the attitude of “I have a responsibility as a devotee.”

## 7. Representing Srila Prabhupada and Krishna

The lecture explains that devotees are representatives of Srila Prabhupada in one sense.

This is why it is said:

> “Your guru will be judged by the way you behave.”

The conditioned soul often thinks in terms of “I” and “mine”:

- My arrangements
- My likes
- My dislikes
- My desires
- My comfort
- My justice

A devotee gradually changes this vision. The devotee begins to think in terms of guru and Krishna.

> “A devotey is different because in that equation it is not I and mind, it's guru and Krishna.”

### Main lesson

The identity of a devotee should influence every area of life—not only temple activities, but also home life, public behavior, work, relationships, and personal habits.

## 8. Krishna Consciousness and Developed Consciousness

One quality of a devotee is seeing Krishna in everything and remaining conscious of Krishna.

The lecture gives the example of Srila Prabhupada observing a tall, old tree. The tree is a living entity with the soul present, but its consciousness is covered and subdued. It experiences the effects of winter, storms, rain, and sunshine, but it does not react in the same way a human being does.

Human life provides a more developed consciousness. We can experience pain, understand consequences, make choices, and consciously change our behavior.

A developed spiritual consciousness means remembering:

- I am a servant of Krishna.
- I am a disciple of Srila Prabhupada.
- My conduct should be exemplary.
- I should remain conscious of Krishna.
- My actions should represent Krishna consciousness.

## 9. Autopilot Mode and Habitual Behavior

Most people live much of their lives in autopilot mode.

Our behavior is influenced by:

- Past experiences
- Upbringing
- Repeated habits
- Mental programming
- The way our brain and neurons have become wired

Examples of habitual responses include:

- Gossiping when there is an opportunity
- Scrolling on the mobile phone during free time
- Reacting automatically without thinking
- Repeating the same behavior even when it is not beneficial

The lecture calls us victims of our habits and programs when we act without conscious choice.

### Main lesson

Spiritual progress requires awareness. We must notice our automatic reactions and consciously choose a better response.

## 10. Animal Instinct and Human Choice

Animals generally act according to instinct and programming.

For example, if a bone is thrown to a dog, the dog will immediately pounce on it. The dog cannot decide to fast today and eat the bone tomorrow. It acts according to instinct.

A tiger eats flesh because it is naturally programmed to do so. It does not receive bad karma for eating its designated food.

Human beings have a higher ability: the ability to choose and discriminate.

### Main lesson

Human life becomes meaningful when we use our freedom of choice to rise above instinct, habit, likes, and dislikes.

## 11. Gradations of Consciousness

The lecture describes a gradual development of consciousness:

1. Animal consciousness
2. Uncultured human consciousness
3. Cultured human consciousness
4. A general belief in God or a higher power
5. Belief in God with worship for personal needs
6. Desire to serve God
7. Complete surrender to God
8. Krishna consciousness

Cultured behavior is an improvement, but culture alone does not necessarily mean God consciousness.

Some people believe that a higher power exists, but their understanding may remain vague. Others worship God mainly to ask for material benefits.

A higher level is to stop trying to order God and instead become a servant of God.

The highest development is complete surrender and Krishna consciousness.

> “As devotees, it's supposed to be Krishna consciousness, the highest evolved consciousness. There's nothing higher than that.”

## 12. Serving God and Accepting Circumstances

At a higher level of consciousness, a person wants to serve God rather than command God.

Complete surrender means accepting that God may maintain us, challenge us, change our plans, or place us in difficult situations.

Even devotees may sometimes ask:

- Why is this happening to me?
- Why should this happen to me?
- I deserve more.
- This was not my mistake.

The lecture explains that this is internal work. Externally, we should do what our duty requires. Internally, we should avoid the mentality of entitlement.

Other people may become instruments through whom we receive what is due to us. Therefore, nobody needs to be treated as a permanent enemy.

### Main lesson

A devotee can perform the required duty while developing inner acceptance, humility, and surrender.

## 13. Arjuna and the Principle of Duty

Arjuna was a warrior, and Krishna instructed him to perform his duty as a kshatriya.

Externally, Arjuna had to fight. Internally, however, he did not want to hurt others. This shows the difference between external action and internal consciousness.

A devotee performs duty because there is a higher purpose—not because of personal anger, revenge, or selfish desire.

### Main lesson

The same action can be performed with different consciousness. The quality of consciousness behind the action is important.

## 14. Spiritual Self-Evaluation

Krishna consciousness is not only a matter of following rules and regulations. Behind the rules is the question of whether our consciousness is actually evolving.

We should regularly ask ourselves:

- Am I becoming purer?
- Am I becoming more conscious of Krishna?
- Am I representing a devotee?
- Are my actions suitable for a devotee?
- Is my behavior suitable for a devotee?
- Is my thinking becoming more spiritual?
- Am I becoming more self-controlled and tolerant?
- Do I remember guru and Krishna, or am I absorbed only in “I” and “mine”?
- Do I react when my surroundings become unclean?
- Am I living according to my spiritual identity?

The purpose of these questions is not to create pride or guilt. They help us honestly understand where we are and what needs to improve.

> “These all questions should go on in our mind. We should evaluate.”

## Final Takeaway

Spiritual dignity means consciously remembering:

- I am a devotee of Krishna.
- I represent Srila Prabhupada.
- My conduct is being observed.
- My habits should not control me.
- My choices should reflect higher consciousness.
- Cleanliness, truthfulness, self-control, tolerance, and humility are part of devotional life.
- I should perform my duty with a higher purpose.
- My consciousness should gradually move from “I and mine” toward guru and Krishna.

The goal is not merely to wear devotional dress or follow external rules. The real goal is to develop the consciousness, behavior, and character of a genuine devotee.
